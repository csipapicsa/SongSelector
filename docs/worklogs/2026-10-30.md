## 2. Benchmark: CNN14 on the CPU, PyTorch vs compiled vs onnxruntime (test only)

Question (chat): is a model faster on the CPU when it runs as C/C++ instead of Python? Wanted as a
test on ~10 random tracks, not in the app.

The app already does this: CNN14 was exported from PyTorch to `cnn14.onnx` and runs in
onnxruntime (C++). The original PyTorch pipeline still exists (MusicFindBoundaries venv, torch
2.5.1 + panns_inference), so both can be measured on the same audio.

- `tests/test_inference_speed.py` (skipped unless `TKC_CNN14_ONNX`, `TKC_TORCH_PYTHON`,
  `TKC_BENCH_TRACKS` are set): picks random tracks from a folder or a `library.sqlite` (read-only),
  decodes them once with the app's `load_audio`, and runs the same chunks through four backends,
  each in its own process with the same threads and one chunk per call. Asserts identical
  embeddings (< 1e-3 from PyTorch) and prints a table.
- `tests/benchmarks/cnn14_backends.py`: standalone worker, also runs under the torch venv.

Result (seed 648823, 10 tracks, 60.8 min of audio, 4 threads):

| backend | load s | model s/track | vs PyTorch | max diff |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PyTorch eager | 6.9 | 10.54 | 1.00x | 0 |
| TorchScript (traced, frozen) | 6.7 | 7.99 | 1.32x | 5.2e-06 |
| onnxruntime, no graph optimisation | 1.1 | 7.26 | 1.45x | 2.0e-05 |
| onnxruntime (as the app) | 1.0 | 7.09 | 1.49x | 2.0e-05 |

Decoding took 8.7 s for all 10. So: yes, ~1.5x on the CPU, and the app already uses the fastest
option. Most of it comes from leaving Python's per-layer dispatch (TorchScript alone gets 1.32x);
onnxruntime's graph optimisations add ~2 %. The convolutions themselves were always C++ in both.
The GPU (0.58 s/track) remains ~12x faster than the best CPU option. One run, backends one after
another; per-track times vary by ~20 %.
